# Decide what a sequence helps the viewer understand

Choose a sequence when change across time is part of what the viewer should see. A page about an ordered mechanism can use an edit to show why one step enables another. A study of atmosphere can use elapsed time without making a causal claim. Compare a still, comparison plate or short cut when any could serve the same purpose.

## Map beats to something the viewer can see

For each beat, write the idea, what changes on screen, what stays stable and how long the viewer can read the result. The moving parts should enact the actual relationship. If the sequence plays the same way for an unrelated organization with only the logo swapped, identify the specific subject that should determine the edit. If shuffling the beats does not change the explanation, you may be showing related examples instead of an ordered mechanism. That can be the right form if that is the actual task.

Put a passage where the reader already has the vocabulary to follow it. For a scroll sequence, keep navigation and native scrolling available. At each important hold the reader needs a complete, settled view. Make the key still work as an opening, a fallback, a reduced view or a reference image as each destination needs. They can use different crops or a separately designed presentation.

## Give movement a job that survives a still comparison

Name what a viewer will recognize, understand or compare. In a causal explanation, join the beats with “because” or “so” and see if reordering changes the claim. In a recognition sequence, state what changes while something familiar stays identifiable. In a comparison, keep alternatives legible as alternatives. The latter two can work without implying cause. A composed hold can be the best way to give observation time.

Before making shots, write the beat's claim, visible verb, subject at rest and way out. Keep a recurring subject, place, arrangement or relationship where it aids recognition. In a metaphor, choose a physical verb that actually enacts the model. Test whether the same sequence could serve an unrelated claim by swapping labels. For an explicitly requested reconstruction, judge what the source asked to preserve instead of applying the swap test as a veto.

Keep a page's own task available. A decision page needs terms and its action, a reference page needs reading and retrieval, and an index needs directly available choices. A sequence within a page must earn the attention it uses. Plan an orientation, a central moment and a settled end that leads somewhere useful. A narrow or interrupted visit may need another edit. Give the still and reduced rendition the same essential meaning and usable access.

## Worked example: show a comparison before the result

A report explains how removing a constraint changes an arrangement. Show the constrained state, make the constraint disappear, then hold the new arrangement beside its previous position. Keep the labels, the part being compared and the viewing direction stable. Ask a viewer to say which constraint changed before showing the prose explanation. If they only infer that something moved, change the beat or use a side-by-side comparison that makes the relation clearer.
