# Review how a draft depends on its sources

Use this guide to judge how a draft relies on source expression and whether its present form has enough independent contribution to retain. Review the complete artifact at an identified version, with section-level findings where needed. This is an editorial assessment; reuse permissions and publication decisions need separate consideration.

## Identify the draft and its sources

In the review, identify the candidate, version, audience and intended use; every known source and the version available to its author; authoring history, drafts, prompts, transformations and contributor roles; quoted and closely adapted passages; claimed independent contribution; and any automated comparison, including its corpus and method. Identify suspected, unavailable, incomplete or unreconstructable sources.

Look beyond the submitted source list for imported templates, earlier drafts, translations, generated intermediates and embedded examples. Precision in a list cannot establish completeness. Treat unavailable sources as evidence gaps.

## Compare the complete arrangement

Compare more than phrases. Inspect chosen and omitted concepts; section, argument and diagnostic order; workflows, checklists, taxonomies and list sequences; examples, tables, warnings, failures and patterns to avoid; distinctive terms, metaphors, labels and explanations; compression, expansion, translation, summary and format changes; source-specific categories and brand substitutions; and how the parts form the final decision, explanation or procedure.

A rewrite can share no long phrase yet keep a source's distinctive selection and arrangement. Synonyms, substituted brands, shuffled sentences, translation or a reformatted list cannot by themselves establish independent derivation.

Shared facts, ordinary methods, necessary interface names and conventional instructions also cannot establish expressive dependence by themselves. Identify what is conventional, then examine the candidate's own choices of problem, distinctions, sequence, examples, trade-offs and explanation.

## Identify the independent contribution

Name and support the contribution: a different decision or audience need, synthesis across independently selected evidence, original distinctions or conditional rules, a structure justified by its purpose, new counterexamples or failure analysis, or observations that change the conclusion or procedure.

Fluency, length, tone changes, generic examples and removed attribution cannot establish that contribution. Distinguish new ideas from new expression. New analysis can coexist with source-dependent passages or structure.

## Use comparison tools as evidence

Automated similarity can find passages, sequences and clusters. It cannot decide whether a fact is conventional, an arrangement distinctive, an omitted source material or a contribution meaningful. Neither zero matching n-grams nor a high similarity score settles originality.

In the review, identify method, corpus, thresholds, exclusions and blind spots. Read important matches in context and compare structure even when the tool finds no overlap. Attribution identifies origin while leaving the question of dependent expression to be assessed.

## Decide what each part needs

Choose the narrowest supported editorial result:

- **Retain as independently expressed:** evidence supports an independent basis for the expression and arrangement.
- **Retain a limited quotation with exact attribution, subject to rights review:** quoted content and origin are clear; permission remains separate.
- **Derive afresh from independently supportable evidence:** keep the problem and facts, then write from a blank page rather than editing the source-shaped arrangement.
- **Remove the current expression:** dependent material has no justified role in the result.
- **Unresolved, awaiting evidence:** missing sources, authoring history or mixed findings prevent a defensible decision.

Assess affected sections separately. An independent appendix cannot settle dependence in the main argument; one conventional phrase does not invalidate an otherwise independent work.

## Return a useful editorial review

Return the candidate, audience and use; known sources, versions and gaps; observations about phrases, structure, sequence, examples, taxonomy and terms; independent contribution and support; whole-artifact and section findings; recommended treatment and reasons; unresolved evidence and stopping conditions; what could justify reconsideration; and whether a rights/use review is needed. Keep these in the requested review rather than a second catalogue.

### Abstract contrast

A draft retains a source's unusual five-stage diagnostic sequence, warnings and example pattern while renaming every stage. That supports a finding of structural dependence even if no long phrase matches. Another draft, built from independently gathered facts, can address the same topic through a different decision, conditional sequence, counterexamples and conclusion. That supports independent expression while leaving rights in assets, quotations and supplied evidence to their separate review.

Hold the affected conclusion when candidate or source version is unknown, sources are materially incomplete, authoring history cannot explain a close structure, quotation and adaptation cannot be identified, or the requested finding requires legal judgment. Resume with missing source bytes, authoring evidence, a revised draft, narrower question or the qualified determination this editorial method cannot supply.

For proposed reuse of a source or component, follow the [source-use rights check](source-rights.md).
