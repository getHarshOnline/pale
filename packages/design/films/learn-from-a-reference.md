# Carry a reference mechanism into a sequence

## Learn the mechanism from the reference

Distinguish inspiration, a selected reconstruction and source reuse. Retain the scoped fidelity of a reconstruction or reuse; inspiration does not make every camera move mandatory. Watch the selected passage at intended speed, then inspect its opening, action, joins, holds and ending. Read its source, prompt or worked construction where available. Missing source leaves the implementation uncertain, even when its appearance is observable.

Notice what persists and what changes: subject, shape, container, viewpoint, environment, material, hierarchy or relation. Study attention, depth, occlusion, reading time, acceleration and sound, rather than reducing the reference to a mood adjective. A contact sheet supports frame inspection, not cadence. Automated cut, motion and lightness measurements locate passages to inspect; they cannot count good transitions or establish taste.

Carry useful observations into the actual shot or edit plan. Keep reference timing distinct from the target's runtime. Preserve accepted decisions during a bounded repair. Do not quietly substitute a shorter study, silence or a simpler treatment for a requested finished result. When a consequential choice remains open, compare credible forms on the same audience outcome; several palettes on the same feature list are not several concepts.

## Worked example

Watch a clip with two objects joining. Inspect the frames where the nearer object occludes the rear object, then play the join at normal speed. Note which shape remains recognizable and when the hold lets the viewer read the relation. If the brief asks to reuse this mechanism, carry the occlusion and readable hold into the shot plan. The new palette is a different choice.
