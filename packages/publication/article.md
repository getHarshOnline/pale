# Write an article from its sources

Use for long-form drafting or revision when the audience, purpose, evidence and voice are known. Develop the reader's question through a traceable line of thought. Research, source authority and permission remain with their owners.

## Establish what this article needs to say

Read the request, corrections, original work and relevant owning account. Reuse an adequate brief. Identify the reader's knowledge, the genre, the intended understanding or decision, and the claims that need support. Keep voice and source authority separate: a voice profile guides expression, not what is true.

Distinguish established facts, interpretation, proposals and uncertainty. Trace material assertions to their support and limits; repeated dependent accounts are not independent evidence. Use the existing source account, citations or working notes. A separate claim ledger is useful only when the complexity of the evidence needs one; ordinary drafting does not require another document. Keep unavailable current facts and unresolved source disagreements visible.

## Develop the thought

Choose an opening that gives the particular subject and stakes. A scene, question, object or thought experiment can help when the source and argument support it; do not invent an anecdote or force a hook onto every piece. Preserve the useful intellectual ambition of the original while correcting its claims. Replacing a difficult argument with generic cautions loses its substance.

Order sections by the reasoning this reader needs. Context, distinction, mechanism, evidence, consequence and action are available movements, not a required sequence or heading template. Use concrete actors, events and examples where they explain the mechanism. Label hypothetical or illustrative examples. Qualify a claim where uncertainty affects its meaning; do not strengthen a forecast, correlation or opinion into a fact.

Keep a claim, explanation, useful example and immediate consequence together when they form one movement. Start a paragraph when the thought, perspective or stage changes. Short paragraphs can mark emphasis; repeated isolated assertions give every sentence the same weight. No sentence quota or paragraph length supplies cohesion. A source-format convention does not determine the argument's rhythm.

Essential definitions, reasoning and qualifications stay in the main text. Companions can develop a derivation, alternative or tangent without repairing an unintelligible main argument. Headings follow the thought. Split a piece when its distinct arguments can stand and connect meaningfully, not merely because it is long. Develop genuine history when it is the subject; keep the editorial reconstruction and private production record out of public narration.

## Revise to the level the work needs

Resolve substantive gaps in argument and source support, then examine structure, repetition, counterevidence and transitions. Apply the selected voice for rhythm, diction, compression and emphasis while retaining necessary depth. Check title and summary fidelity, useful references and destination constraints. Reuse valid review and stop after the finite revision needed for the requested artifact. Separate a source problem from a cosmetic improvement.

Return the draft with material unresolved evidence or decisions and an explanation of consequential adaptations when needed. Extra briefs, ledgers and revision reports are not default deliverables. Hold only the affected work when a central claim lacks support, rights or authority are unresolved, or an actual publication requires an owner decision. Native publication and approval remain separate.

## Before handing off

- [ ] Put the reader’s question and main claim where they can find them.
- [ ] Keep support, context and examples beside the claims they serve.
- [ ] Read the delivered argument in order.
