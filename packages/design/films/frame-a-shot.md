# Plan a shot from both ends

Sketch one rough frame per beat and per transition. Keep the beat map nearby and step through the rough frames at the intended pace. If the viewer cannot read the cause, the order or the hold at this stage, resolve that before making the media. Compare thumbnails when the direction is open. A repair to an agreed sequence can start with its existing choices.

| In the shot record | Decide |
| --- | --- |
| Beat and viewer task | What becomes understood or perceptible here. Name the source and what it contributes. |
| Treatment, and the simpler one it beat | The chosen construction and why it serves the beat. When adding substantial machinery, compare a simpler viable alternative. Preserve the requested fidelity of a reconstruction. |
| Subject and scale | What owns attention. A wide shot places it, a medium shot shows an action, a detail shows its consequential material fact. Hold one scale for a comparison if changing it would disguise the difference. |
| Action and timing | The main verb, then each supporting action. Mark launch, travel, arrival, settle and the reading hold. Weight, distance, framing and sound all affect duration. |
| Camera | A push reveals a useful detail, a pull restores context, a tilt joins ground to sky, and a focus change moves attention between planes. A locked camera can carry complex action. |
| Depth and words | What the foreground frames, what the subject does, what the ground establishes, and where the words remain readable. Count required reads, including screenshot text, captions and labels; give the beat one primary task and enough time at the smallest intended display size. |
| Entry, exit and light | Design both ends of the cut. Keep the recurring object's scale, color and material, the direction of travel and the key light unless their change is the beat. |

Put the shot record with its editable source. A generated shot's first and last frames can come from it. An authored scene can keep editable endpoints in the timeline. Mark where a title or control has to remain readable in the destination's player. A page camera moves the image under its DOM text. For a film, open ground, a deliberate mask, or depicted interface text can each carry the read. Check legibility throughout the hold and at crossings.

Check the original lighting of supplied footage before grading a join. When screen direction matters, a subject exiting right can enter the next frame from the left. A reversal may communicate a turn, but the cut needs to give the viewer a way to recognize it. Reader-driven camera movement should stay proportional to input. Avoid a sudden full-field spin or zoom in response to a small scroll.

## Worked example

| Field | View of a door mechanism |
| --- | --- |
| Subject and action | The latch retracts. The door pivots after the latch clears. |
| Scale and camera | Locked medium view for the two parts, then detail for the contact. |
| Depth and words | Caption on still ground. The hinge never passes over it. |
| Entry and exit | Enter at the visible latch. Leave on the open gap with the light and travel direction unchanged. |
| Timing | Let the latch finish before the door moves. Hold on the gap long enough to read the explanation. |

Play the two sketches in order. If the opening appears to pull the latch rather than follow it, change the timing and inspect the frames again.
