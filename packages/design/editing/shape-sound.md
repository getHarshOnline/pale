# Shape sound around what the viewer hears and sees

## Shape sound to the selected treatment

Select silence, effects, music, narration or a mix from the brief. No voiceover does not imply silence; every click need not receive an effect. Stage sonic hierarchy alongside visual attention. Align an effect's audible onset or peak to its intended cause, not merely its file start. Compare the decoded mix with retained stems and a gain-matched source when diagnosing a quiet effect.

Inspect speech, music bed, peaks and meaningful dynamic contrast separately. A plausible compressor setting can make the bed inaudible. A waveform or loudness number cannot establish a good mix; listen with the picture. Choose delivery targets from the actual destination rather than one channel's preset. At a held result, consider when picture, speech and caption settle and when the next sound and picture arrive. A shorter hold or sound leading a cut can improve flow while preserving reading time and intentional silence.

## Worked example

A recorded click lands at a visual change. Align its audible peak with the visible action, then listen to the encoded result at intended level. Compare with a gain-matched cut without the effect. If the music bed buries a quiet consonant, change the mix and listen through the join again. Keep the brief’s intended silence where it carries the transition.
