# Review an editorial draft

Read the current artifact, original request and corrections, accepted sources and the reader's purpose. Reuse the existing brief and evidence. Check material claims, including whether an excerpt, teaser or destination makes sense alone. Establish source identity, publisher choice, editorial placement and intended route separately. Similar names or themes do not establish who owns the source.

Read as the audience would, without supplying missing explanation. Can the reader understand the claim, perform the task or use the reference at the required depth? Give concrete findings tied to passages, consequences and proposed corrections. Preserve useful examples, reasoning, voice and qualifications while removing process clutter. Shorter text alone cannot establish clarity.

For a material question about source dependence or permitted use, choose `source-expression` or `source-rights` from `pale.research`. Their findings serve different purposes; ordinary copy review need not invoke both. Send unresolved permission or ownership questions to the responsible owner and leave dependent claims open even if a specialist assessment was not selected beforehand. Return candidate identity, relevant findings, unverified claims or rights and the decision still needed.

Keep editorial review, local build checks and release approval distinct. When a claim depends on current publication state, request the owning system's action record and independently read back that state.

Use [documentation guidance](documentation.md) for help, developer explanation or reference; [staged reader review](reveals.md) for reveal order, clues or first-read inference; and [reviewed-asset checks](reviewed-media.md) for archive installation or asset selection. Choose each for the question it answers.

## Before handing off

- [ ] Was the current draft read for its audience and purpose?
- [ ] Does each material finding identify a passage and consequence?
- [ ] Are specialist findings and owner decisions distinct?
