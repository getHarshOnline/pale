# Keep a candidate's review history

Separate production from evaluation when shared assumptions would make self-review materially less trustworthy. Separation reduces that bias; quality still needs evidence.

Before evaluation, fix the brief, permissions, available evidence, blocking criteria, rubric, budgets and candidate identity. Use deterministic checks for mechanical requirements. The generator produces a candidate within scope and answers prior findings; its rationale is not evidence that the result works. The evaluator receives the fixed candidate, applies a rubric with concrete examples, cites evidence for each criterion and leaves the artifact unchanged.

## Match the evidence to the criterion and candidate

Distinguish the original brief, reference material, review before authoring, execution receipts and observations of the resulting candidate. A plan review establishes something about the plan. A receipt establishes the work and checks it records. A criterion about the delivered result needs an observation of that exact result, identifying the candidate and what was inspected. A reference can set the comparison standard; evidence of meeting it must concern the candidate itself.

For each finding, identify the criterion, candidate, evidence, observer or check, and scope inspected. Renaming a file, copying a judgment or moving the same record adds no observation or independence. To carry a result across revisions, show what remained equivalent for that criterion and preserve the original evidence identity. Missing coverage or identity leaves the criterion unverified and any dependent blocking condition open.

## Preserve the decision across rounds

Keep the review history: candidate versions, findings, responses, evidence changes, rejected paths and the reason for another revision. Each revised artifact is a new candidate. Keep the rubric stable after scores arrive, blocking failures separate from averages, dissent visible and abandoned paths identifiable when they informed the next choice.

Continue while evidence shows acceptance gaps shrinking or new evidence could change a material judgment. Stop on acceptance, exhausted budget, repeated lack of progress, conflicting authority, unavailable evidence or a candidate that needs actions beyond the permitted scope.

Return the candidate history, results by criterion, responses to findings, unresolved claims, dissent and a recommendation to accept, revise, stop or escalate. The engine or owning system controls invocation, isolation, iteration state, artifact edits and consequential actions.
