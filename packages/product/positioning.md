# Review how an offer speaks to its audience

Use this method when a product or initiative needs positioning and copy that helps its intended audience decide what to do. Read the supplied offer, product evidence, audience research, competitors, brand voice, publication destination and permitted actions. Read current market, audience, policy, product and operating facts from those sources, keeping changing facts with their owners. Use real support for customer proof, urgency, scarcity, outcomes and market facts; leave unsupported claims out. Work within the assigned subject and the caller’s permissions.

For an independent review, give the reviewer the product scope, available voice guidance, evidence against the proposal and permitted actions. Persuasive wording must stay within what the evidence supports.

## Explain why this audience would choose the offer

Identify the audience's situation, current alternative, desired progress, objections and the evidence they need to believe the claim. State the positioning as a choice: for this audience and task, why choose this offer over the real alternative, and which proof supports that choice? Keep the central promise, the features and the proof distinct.

Build the message in an order that works across channels: the main outcome, how it is achieved, what makes the offer different, supporting evidence, answers to objections and a next step. Adapt length and structure to the destination while keeping the claim intact. Make each call to action an explicit request whose consequence matches the reader's commitment. A sale must be recognisable as a sale, and a click that collects data must say so.

## Return the copy with its support and open questions

Check the draft against its sources, brand voice, audience understanding, privacy, applicable rules and the current page or channel where it will appear. Cite the source or observation behind each material recommendation. Return the positioning, message order, copy variants, support for each claim, assumptions and any tests or owner decisions still needed. Give the owner a diagnosis, strategy or proposed artifact they can act on; if the evidence does not support a message, explain which claim is still missing it. Publishing, targeting an audience, spending, contacting people, changing configuration or accounts and taking other actions require the owner’s permission through the tool that performs them.

## Before handing off

- [ ] Does the message address a named audience, task and real alternatives?
- [ ] Is each promised difference supported by evidence?
- [ ] Are unresolved claims clearly held out of the finished copy?
