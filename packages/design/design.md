# Carry meaning from the source to the rendered design

Design is a set of working methods for turning a subject and an intended experience into a visible, usable result. The brief, accepted direction and selected references supply its character. These methods help construct and judge that result; they do not impose one palette, material metaphor, page template or story form on every project.

Think of the design system as a compiler of meaning: source meaning → design intent → shared roles and tokens → components and composition → motion and graphics → rendered output. The chain explains where decisions belong. A token holds a shared design value; it does not generate a fact, replace an authored asset or require every piece of scene geometry to become a token.

1. **Meaning stays intact.** Preserve identity, values, units, qualifications, sources, rights, status, ownership and destinations as the form changes. Presentation resolves meaning; it never invents the facts needed to fill a composition.
2. **Shared behavior has one owner.** Keep access, focus, dismissal, feedback and recurring interaction rules consistent within the system that owns them. A shared component or runtime contract carries those rules to its consumers; local styling must not quietly change their behavior.
3. **Identity has more than one axis.** Type, geometry, composition, material, light and motion can make a treatment recognizable. Color is one coordinate. Shared mechanics do not require every subject to look alike, and a new palette alone does not establish a distinct identity.
4. **Restraint keeps intended presence.** Control competing emphasis without deleting the character, atmosphere or expressive moment the brief asks for. Density comes from hierarchy and grouping, and space has a purpose. Where the brief declares an expressive range, meet its minimum presence and stay within its maximum intensity; falling below the floor fails too. These local limits are neither quotas for effects nor permission to make the work empty.
5. **Design the supported forms.** Light, dark, reduced motion, narrow and wide layouts, input and capability fallbacks preserve the task and meaning through deliberate composition. Use the product's actual supported combinations. A publication's complete static document and an application's honest unavailable state answer different contracts.
6. **Choose the medium and clock deliberately.** Reader input, a played response, a slow living field and an authored film have different timing and delivery needs. Reuse a source's useful construction while preserving its selected role. Engine constraints govern execution; an engine's aesthetic defaults do not replace the brief.
7. **Inspect what was made.** A specification, source check, isolated specimen, rendered result, completed interaction and owner approval establish different things. Look at the actual consuming surface, compare it with the intended result and fix defects where they originate. Passing checks does not supply a creative verdict or approval to publish.

When requirements seem to conflict, protect source meaning and access, establish the actual runtime constraint, then resolve identity and local expression from the accepted brief. Preserve both sides of a real design tension while comparing treatments. A page-specific judgment does not become a universal rule, and a small repair does not reopen settled direction. Use existing authorization and hold only work that depends on a genuinely unresolved owner decision.
