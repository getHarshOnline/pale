# Judge the design through its working parts

After checking ordinary failures, ask both questions: could this serve an unrelated subject with only the words changed, and does the whole page or film reach the standard of the selected references? The first tests whether the visual argument belongs to the subject; the second tests the intended quality. Familiar controls and an explicitly chosen reconstruction can rightly retain their form. Neither novelty nor restraint is a verdict by itself.

Look at the artifact and record what it communicates. Then read the original request, corrections and reference roles, and write the verdict against those sources. For a rendered review, inspect the builder's plan and detector output afterward to diagnose or reconcile findings; do not let that explanation stand in for what the artifact communicates. A plan review assesses the actual plan and original sources, leaving rendered behavior unproved.

Use the lenses the decision needs and report what was actually inspected. A full design review covers all applicable lenses; a focused repair keeps its affected questions and earlier findings. Silence is not a pass. A quiet derivative cannot satisfy a required richer treatment merely because it matches its own narrower plan. A reconstruction needs faithful transfer; a sequence needs its chosen relation, whether causal change, recognition, comparison or contemplation.

## Challenge the verdict before returning it

1. Argue the opposite verdict from the actual captures or playback, then answer that case against the original brief. Check that a finding is not merely a preference for your own look or for less presence.
2. Try to falsify the page's claims: repeat an action, arrive through another route, remove a supported capability, or compare the other intended context. Record the result that holds or the defect it exposes.
3. After a fix, repeat the failed checks and the sweep and design questions the change can affect. End when the scoped pass finds no new defect. If fixes stop converging, reconsider the cause and keep the unresolved finding open.
4. Reopen a finding when the evidence that closed it is superseded, disappears or now skips the relevant check. A contrary observation of the real delivered page reopens the affected verdict.

## Worked example

A page passes its link, focus and overflow checks, but every section uses the same card composition. Compare what the primary claim needs with what the supporting records need. If their different roles are invisible, repair hierarchy and composition. If repeated cards are the intended comparison, retain them and judge whether their differences can be read. The repeated shape is evidence to examine, not the failure itself.
