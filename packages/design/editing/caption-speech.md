# Keep speech, captions and explanatory graphics aligned

## Make speech, captions and graphics agree

Choose transcription for the actual language and selected model. One model's failure does not establish that the language is unsupported everywhere. Check segment boundaries, restarts, omitted words and translated material against the audio. Windowed transcription needs offset reconstruction and boundary overlap review. Use matching that supports the source's Unicode text.

Keep spoken wording, display correction and factual correction distinct. Fixing a spelling in captions does not silently correct what a speaker said or turn a wrong number into a true one. Phrase-led graphics and word-aligned captions serve different reading tasks. Give explanatory text its own read window instead of copying the speed of speech onto every graphic.

Validate the current timed track and inspect it as drawn in the actual player. A loaded cue can clip, ignore positioning or cover essential picture detail. Check long cues, operative labels, inline and fullscreen views as applicable, and controls visible and hidden. Inspect burned-in captions in the pixels. Retain complete words when adjusting boxes, position or line breaks. Captions, transcript and an audience description of essential visuals have different roles; a reviewer's notes cannot stand in for the audience alternative.

## Worked example

A caption spans a join and a generated word misses the first consonant after it. Listen through the cut, remap the cue to the new interval and inspect the longest line in the actual player with controls visible. If a diagram needs time to be understood, leave its explanatory label up beyond the spoken phrase when the scene can support it.
