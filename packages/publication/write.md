# Compose content from accepted sources

Turn source material into the requested content while preserving the author's meaning, evidence and established voice.

## Establish the source and purpose

Identify the available sources, audience, communication objective, reader question, medium, voice Pack or profile, and claims or disclosures that must remain exact. Keep missing proof visible.

Read the original request and corrections with the owning account and intended audience's existing copy. Distinguish source statements, interpretations and defaults. Reuse an adequate brief. A reference can demonstrate a particular quality without establishing product facts or permission to reuse its assets.

## Develop a coherent artifact

Find the defensible claims, observations, examples or scenes in the sources. Choose a governing idea and a structure suited to the medium. Compress only where claims remain true; expand explanation without inventing evidence. Preserve characteristic expression where it carries meaning and avoid generic platform habits that displace substance.

For several outputs, derive each from the same sources and supported claims rather than repeatedly adapting a draft. Keep the source trace in the existing brief or owning account. Identify support for material assertions and significant changes in order, density or presentation required by the medium.

## Preserve the subject's scope

For a product, distinguish overview, feature demonstration and operating tutorial before choosing the example. An overview explains the audience's job, what the product enables and the capabilities that make that credible. One governing idea can connect several necessary parts. An easily demonstrated feature can support this account while leaving the whole product's purpose intact.

Choose detail that helps the audience understand or trust the result. Keep related capability groups connected and explain a deliberate deferral in the companion material where needed. Put current usability limits beside the affected capability. A closing development note cannot qualify earlier instructions that imply the product works today. Honour a request for one feature without expanding it into a launch. A tutorial needs observations supporting its actual steps.

The owner determines where source knowledge lives, where writing belongs and which public route presents it. The draft remains a candidate for the required review and checks.

Return the requested content with material evidence gaps and consequential adaptations the recipient needs to understand. Keep the idea and source trace in the existing brief; add worksheets or rationale only when the task uses them. Finish when the complete thought is clear, assertions supported and voice appropriate. Distribution sequencing belongs to crosspost guidance; publication and platform state belong to their operating systems and owners.

## Before handing off

- [ ] Does the draft serve its reader and intended action?
- [ ] Do sources, rights and open questions remain with the relevant claims?
- [ ] Has the draft been read, with remaining review identified?
