# Review a site's search visibility

Review an identified page or site for a defined search objective. Identify the publication, canonical route, rendered artifact, audience question and evidence sought. Read its editorial commitments and intended reader outcome before choosing what to improve. Useful reading, discovery, citation and an authorised enquiry can serve different publications. Read the actual output before interpreting diagnostics. Check current search-engine behaviour, index state, traffic, queries, competitors and site configuration from evidence. These observations cannot promise a ranking.

## Establish what the evidence can answer

Use the audience method to choose the organising inquiry and distinguish its supporting questions from separate tasks. Start with the current page and its place in the publication, including existing work that must be finished before new topics are commissioned. Query observations can challenge that structure; they do not automatically replace its purpose.

Keep source scope with each finding. Search Console query impressions describe the site's observed visibility, not total market searches. A keyword provider estimates demand and difficulty using its own definitions. A chosen prompt run shows a response under those conditions; it does not count how often the public asks that question. Check current provider documentation for the report, dimensions, sampling, omissions and time lag actually available. Verify access and export or API support separately from an advertised capability. Do not infer unobserved questions from an empty report or call missing data zero.

## Diagnose the obstacle before editing

Trace the path to visibility: the URL must be discoverable, fetchable, renderable, eligible for indexing, understandable and useful for the query. Inspect relevant canonical and redirect relationships, robots and sitemap signals, status codes, internal links, rendered content, page purpose, duplication, structured data, language and locale relationships, performance and accessibility. Distinguish technical obstacles from content, authority, demand and measurement problems.

For each finding, identify the URL or template, observation, affected stage, consequence, confidence and smallest correction. Check source and rendered behaviour. A scanner warning points to something to inspect; an indexing request cannot establish inclusion. A valid link or sitemap establishes its corresponding output behaviour. Index submission and deployment remain separate actions. Prioritise by reach, relevance to the publication's purpose, severity and recoverability.

## Revise the answer where the reader needs it

When the evidence suggests a content gap, read the passage in the whole argument. Check whether it answers the main inquiry before adding follow-ups. A query may expose a missing explanation, a misleading title, a different audience or a separate task. Choose the correction that serves that need: clarify the existing answer, add a supporting section or example, link a suitable destination, or propose a separate piece. Preserve useful original work and stable routes. More queries and more headings alone do not make a better page.

Apply [editorial review](edit.md) to the changed draft. Where a section offers an answer or reference that may be read alone, keep its subject, conditions and source support intelligible without requiring the rest of the article to repair the claim. Use a question heading when it helps readers recognise their question; retain a different heading or narrative sequence when it better serves the work. Put relevant evidence beside the answer, with dates, units, population and qualifications where they affect its meaning. A statistic earns its place by supporting a claim, not by making the passage look authoritative.

Choose a table for a real comparison, a diagram for a relationship, an example for a difficult application and prose for the reasoning each needs. Preserve accessible labels and the same facts in visible and structured output. Concise summaries can help a reader find an answer, but neither a table nor question-shaped headings establish that a chatbot will cite it. Keep the publication's genre, voice, argument and depth while making the particular answer clearer.

## Compare the revision with the right baseline

Use the research updates method to assess new observations against the accepted claim, and the comparison method when deciding whether a change improved an outcome. Identify the actual published revision and release date, eligible pages and audience, baseline, primary measure and observation window before interpreting results. Keep query, country, device and other relevant groupings comparable. Changes in the mix of queries can change an aggregate rank or click rate without improving the original query's result.

An observation before and after an edit can suggest a useful next step. Causal credit needs a comparison that addresses other changes, seasonality, indexing delays and measurement differences. Assigning visitors to versions can test a reader action; it does not by itself isolate search-ranking effects. A search experiment may compare suitable groups of pages, with sufficient observations and credible controls. Choose the assignment and analysis unit for the question, preserving crawler access and checking the search provider's current testing rules. Do not call a few unrelated pages a controlled experiment.

Keep search visibility, sampled answer-engine mentions or citations, visits, action starts and completed outcomes separate. Attribute a later enquiry only when the permitted records actually connect it to the relevant page or visit. A missing join leaves that attribution open. A review cadence is a chance to inspect evidence, not a deadline for improvement: report insufficient evidence when the observations cannot distinguish a useful effect from noise. There is no fixed period after which a page must rank.

Return what changed, what the comparison establishes, its limits and the next justified action. Keep a page correction with its source, a proposed coverage change with the publication's editorial owner, and a reusable-method proposal with the method's owner and applicable evaluation and adoption process. A successful edit on one page does not license rewriting every publication or adopting a learned policy automatically. The caller supplies the schedule, sources, permitted actions and result destination; this review does not activate tools or publish its own recommendations.

## Worked example: help a founder shortlist investors

> [!NOTE]
>
> **Hypothetical example.** This publication and review have no measured search volumes or observed improvement.

Suppose a matching platform helps seed-stage founders find investors suited to their companies. Its organising inquiry is “How do I find investors who fit my seed-stage startup?” Sector, stage, geography, investment range and whether a fund leads a round help answer it. Negotiating a term sheet is a later task deserving a linked guide. The cluster describes the reader's needs; it does not determine the site's folder structure.

Apply the practitioner's supplied keyword criteria to an authorised, dated export for the intended market. If no thresholds or export are available, leave demand unmeasured and offer the grouping as a hypothesis. A phrase about startup jobs can pass volume and difficulty filters yet fail the fundraising intent. Explain that exclusion rather than promoting it because its numbers look attractive.

Suppose a later page-level query report contains sector-fit questions, while the guide merely says “choose relevant investors.” Inspect the passage before concluding that those impressions justify a new article. The missing help is how to assess fit, which belongs within the existing shortlist task. A proposed replacement could read:

> Build your shortlist by checking each investor's published sector, stage and geographic criteria against your startup. Then check whether the stated investment range and role in a round match what you seek. Record when you checked and leave unpublished criteria unknown; a plausible match does not mean the investor will invest.

A compact comparison gives the reader a way to apply that answer:

| Fit question | Evidence to check | What remains uncertain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does the fund cover this sector and stage? | Its current investment criteria; portfolio examples can provide context. | Portfolio history alone does not establish its current mandate. |
| Does its investment range fit the round? | Its published range, currency and stated conditions, with the date checked. | An unpublished range stays unknown; a stated range is not an offer. |
| Can it invest in this location and lead the round? | Its current geographic criteria and stated role. | Eligibility or willingness may need direct confirmation. |

On a real page, source the actual investor claims and retain their qualifications. Add an original worked shortlist if evidence permits, without inventing financial figures. Review the page for readability and accurate structured output, then leave publication to its authorised owner.

Compare the published revision against the declared baseline for the same eligible audience and query group. Check whether relevant discovery and the page's permitted next action changed, accounting for other releases and reporting lag. With sparse traffic, the honest result may be that the answer is clearer but its effect on search remains unknown. An apparent improvement can justify another comparison that tests the specific finding; it does not establish automatic rankings for supporting queries.

Return inspected scope, evidence, prioritised findings, areas with no issue found, measurement gaps and proposed validation. Hold an affected recommendation when site or analytics ownership is unclear, current index evidence is missing, the tactic is deceptive or the change needs unapproved content, routing, publication or provider actions.

## Before handing off

- [ ] Does the proposed correction serve the publication's organising inquiry and preserve the useful original work?
- [ ] Were the intended route, rendered answer and relevant discovery configuration inspected?
- [ ] Are observations, estimates, comparisons and remaining uncertainties distinguished?
- [ ] Does the result leave publication, coverage and reusable-method decisions with their respective owners?
