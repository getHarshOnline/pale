# Check the media version shown in a publication

## Identify the asset and placement

In the placement review, identify destination, slot, source asset revision, permission for this use and the asset review. State its role in the surrounding content. Keep original, derived renditions and placements separately identifiable. A crop, encoding, poster, caption or fallback has its own identity. Specify permitted variants and transformations, conditions that choose them and meaning or detail that must survive.

## Inspect what the reader receives

Use authorised tools for build and destination observations.

Follow build and theme or player configuration to the generated resource address. In a fresh destination context, inspect the resource actually selected, its bytes or system identity, selection conditions and surrounding presentation. An inventory can establish that the intended asset exists while another valid rendition appears. Test a wrong available rendition and a missing intended rendition separately.

Choose conditions that can change this placement: viewport and density, controls and captions, reduced motion, disabled scripts or print. Check crop and alignment around the subject and whether fallback preserves required meaning. Include an asset that loads correctly but has important detail hidden by clipping or an overlay. Leave untested conditions open. The owner supplies intended renditions, thresholds and destination-specific checks.

## Separate observation and acceptance

Give the independent reviewer the original brief and assembled placement. Tie findings to the tested build, rendition and condition. After replacement, recheck affected presentation while preserving the scope of earlier observations. Build output, visual acceptance, permission for the use and reading back a published result answer different questions. The publisher owns selection, release and its record.

- [ ] Are the review result and open placement or approval questions clear?
