# Reveal more without hiding the task

Put secondary detail beside the control that names it. Keep the direct route available on touch, keyboard and pointer.

Disclose secondary detail beside its named trigger. Essential qualifications, required fields and the way forward remain available without a preview. A preview is a view of its destination, not a second place to invent or maintain claims. Preserve the direct destination on touch; provide a separate accessible preview action only where useful. The accessibility prerequisite owns dismissal, persistence and hover/focus conditions.

Use built-in disclosure where it fits. Preserve relevant open state through local navigation; use an addressed page when nested disclosure becomes hard to follow. Do not impose an accordion that closes other answers unless the task requires that behavior. Keep required no-script reading and search paths working.

For a long ordered set, consider addressed pagination with a clear position and Back behavior. An editorial subset needs a clear link to the complete set. Explicit expansion can work when it preserves focus, reading position and a way back. Tables or code may need local scrolling rather than page-wide horizontal overflow. Neither infinite scroll nor arbitrary truncation follows from a wish to keep the first view short. Check where the reader lands after content grows.

## Worked example: preview a related article

Use the article title as a real link. A separate preview action can show an image and summary for a reader who wants them. Keep essential qualifications and the direct link available without the preview. Try keyboard dismissal, touch and a screen reader. If the preview obscures content, follow the hover and focus requirements for dismissal, persistence and pointer travel. If three disclosures must be opened to reach the answer, give it an addressed page instead.
