# Develop a voice profile from original work

A voice profile describes repeatable editorial choices found in identified sources. Use it when several writers or formats need consistency. A logo, adjectives or generic positioning cannot establish a speaker's voice.

## Choose and understand the sources

Define speaker, audience, purpose, languages, period and media. Give more weight to owner-approved first-person work and repeatedly published originals than campaign copy, third-party descriptions or isolated experiments. Attribute differences between speakers or eras. Establish the identity and scope of newly retrieved material before including it.

Use varied samples to distinguish lasting choices from topic and format. Examine sentence shape, pacing, compression, vocabulary, formality, point of view, use of evidence, humour, emotional range, metaphor, calls to action, formatting and transitions. In the proposed profile, record consistent absences as well as recurring choices.

## Describe choices that help revise a draft

Turn observations into rules supported by examples and counterexamples. Explain what a quality such as directness does to an opening, claim, qualification or sentence. Distinguish choices that travel across media, adjustments for a medium, topic-specific vocabulary and tentative patterns needing more evidence.

Keep facts and audience needs intact even when a sample is rhetorically bold. Use characteristic choices to carry meaning instead of imitating catchphrases.

## Test the proposed profile

Apply it to a neutral paragraph and one real destination format. Compare both with source material set aside from the derivation. Check whether they make similar choices without copying phrases or exaggerating surface quirks. Ask the owner to identify false matches or missing tensions and revise from those corrections.

Return the source ranking, observed patterns, governing choices, medium adjustments, imitations to avoid, uncertainties and test examples in the proposed profile. Leave it unresolved when sources cannot be authenticated, are too sparse, are dominated by ghostwriting, contradict one another without clear attribution or are unavailable for the intended use.

The accepted profile and approved examples belong in a Pack or the owner's private source. Samples, platform constraints, approvals and publication actions retain their own homes and owners.

## Before handing off

- [ ] Were owner-approved samples read in context?
- [ ] Are reusable voice choices supported with useful examples?
- [ ] Do local conventions stay local when they do not transfer?
