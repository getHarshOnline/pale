# Study a complete reference before carrying its craft into the cut

Watch the complete reference in its delivery context, including its sound. Watch the relevant passage again to understand where attention goes, where the eye finds the subject after a join and when it has time to read. Note any motion, silence, held frame or material change that performs part of the argument. A screenshot of a peak cannot describe its setup or what follows it.

Give each reference its role. An inspiration can contribute a relation or a pace. A reconstruction has to preserve the agreed treatment at the agreed fidelity. A reusable source can provide an actual mechanism or editable material. When two roles share a clip, link the relevant span separately to each decision. Keep the source, observed time range and what survives, changes or falls away with the edit that uses it.

Use measured holds, positions and speeds to locate an observation. They do not tell you whether the cut reads. When the choice is open, compare genuinely different treatments against the same outcome. A locked view, an object transformation, spatial travel, a typographic argument and observational footage ask different things of the viewer. Use comparable opening, central change and payoff. Show them at intended scale and pace and compare where the energy changes and what the viewer can explain. Keep the existing identity when the unresolved decision concerns only the form.

## Worked example

A reference uses a locked frame to make a tool's small state change obvious, then pulls back to reveal its effect. Study the lock, the first readable result and the pullback together. In the new cut, a side-by-side close view may show the change more clearly than a pullback. Keep a common edge through both views and ask a viewer where the result became apparent. If the brief called for reconstruction of the pullback, keep that move and fix its timing or framing within the agreed scope.
