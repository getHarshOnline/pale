# Compare the alternatives a customer can choose

Read the decision to be made, customer choice, segment, time horizon, supplied candidates and existing evidence. Use the research method throughout. First decide which alternatives belong in the comparison, explain exclusions, and account for differences by segment and time. Then define the comparisons that matter to the decision, put claims on a common basis and test the emerging explanation against contrary evidence.

Use current public evidence supplied through the task's permitted research tools. Distinguish reported, observed, independently supported, inferred and unresolved claims. Qualitative labels can organise judgment; present measured rankings only when the measures support them.

Carry the same comparison unit from the candidate list into the evidence for each claim. An analogous service in another channel may illustrate an idea while leaving open whether customers can substitute it today or whether it is deployed. When amounts, units, timing or conditions conflict, keep the mismatch visible and name the source or observation needed to resolve it.

Return the chosen alternatives and reasons for including or excluding each, comparison definitions, evidence, contradictions, fields that cannot be compared, information that may be out of date, and what this means for the decision. For each critical unknown, name the smallest observation that would settle it. Finish when every material hypothesis is supported, contradicted or explicitly unresolved. This analysis does not contact competitors, buy access, publish claims or change products or market records.
