# Compare new evidence with an accepted baseline

## Start from the subject as it is now

For a recurring watch, read the subject’s owning pages, existing research and sources, relevant code and open work before choosing the question. Separate its accepted position, intended design, configured state and observed results. A paused programme can still be watched; a research finding does not resume its implementation.

Find sources by their identity, path or alias, following the current owner’s index when they move. A changed heading or digest is a reason to inspect the new version, not a reason to abandon the research. Treat edits to an owner page as new input, preserving the previous observation while checking what changed. A content hash can identify saved bytes; it cannot decide which version governs the subject today.

The workspace supplies the subject, question, schedule, permitted sources and result destination. This method supplies the research procedure. Scheduling, execution and saving a note remain the caller’s responsibilities.

## Establish the accepted claim

Identify the claim and baseline version, owner, previous evidence, conditions that make a change material and possible decisions. Distinguish the accepted position, proposed interpretations and earlier observations. Before screening new material, state what would strengthen, narrow, contradict or leave the claim intact. If the baseline cannot be identified, preserve the new evidence and hold the update.

## Compare source changes and applicability

Distinguish an independent origin, a revision or correction to the same origin, and a new location or commentary. Identify each revision and compare the actual passage, population, units, time and method. A correction can invalidate a claim without adding an independent source. In the review, identify claims that relied on superseded evidence and the correction's scope.

Compare direct follow-up with the strongest available contradictory or null finding. Examine neglected populations, alternative explanations or a plausible reverse causal direction when they could change the decision. Use another domain's work when the mapping and a test that could disprove it are explicit. Novelty, recency, citation counts and resemblance alone cannot change an accepted claim. Identify gaps and inaccessible counterevidence.

## Change the angle deliberately

Start with roughly 60% of search effort on the current question and dominant explanation, 25% on its strongest rival, simpler substitute or negative result, and 15% outside the current frame. Adjust to the question and evidence. These are effort weights, not quotas for findings: a weak result deserves neither a stronger conclusion nor space in the main answer merely to fill a category.

Use the existing research and recent watch results to choose an underused challenge:

- **The existing approach may be enough.** Compare ordinary configuration, a native feature, a managed service, a mature framework or a simpler workflow.
- **The buyer may have no reason to change.** Look for absent budget, low urgency, poor margins, a long sales cycle, support costs or a preference that defeats the offer.
- **The cause may run the other way.** Test selection bias, accounting timing, survivorship, measurement error or another cause of the observed result.
- **The idea may have failed before.** Study adoption, integration, maintenance, governance, liability and institutional barriers in earlier attempts.
- **Another problem may matter more.** Compare a different buyer, segment, research branch or system boundary.
- **Another field may expose the limit.** Name the source and target objects, what relationship survives the mapping and how to test it. Resemblance alone carries no conclusion.
- **The strongest test may refute the idea.** Seek the observation or experiment most likely to overturn the current explanation.

Use the last seven completed watch results to favour the least-used angle, then the least recently used; use the order above to break a remaining tie. Avoid the same angle for more than two consecutive runs. Keep the chosen challenge and any consequential coverage gap with the watch’s existing result so the next run can choose differently. An unsuccessful search supplies no support for the claim and needs no separate diary. When there is no previous history, start with the challenge most likely to change the answer.

Search original research, corrections, replications and useful technical discussion. Check whether the recent evidence is dominated by one author, vendor, dataset or publication. Compare announced capability with shipped use and measured outcomes, internal architecture with independent users, and category demand with residual pain, willingness to pay and voluntary repeat use. Include relevant local and international evidence. Gaps change where effort goes; they never lower the standard for a finding.

Before proposing a new research branch, require a strong primary result or independent credible corroboration, an explicit mapping to this question, a consequence that matters, a possible falsifier and a simpler baseline. One programme may lend another a question or a dimension to examine; it cannot lend an unproved conclusion.

Ask what the search was trying to confirm, which source class dominates, what would make the position wrong and whether more research is avoiding an available experiment or outcome check. If repetition is producing no new observation, make the next useful step a counter-source search or that check rather than another similar brief.

## Connect evidence across the claimed stages

Trace observed input to the decision-relevant outcome. For each transition, define what counts as reaching the next stage and identify entity, event, observation window, unit and denominator. A stage label establishes the wording used. Check its definition and underlying records before treating a new label on the same number as movement through the process.

Join successive stages with stable identifiers or independently checked linkage. Align eligibility, time windows, weights and measurement unit. Account for one-to-many events, repeated observations, expiry and unmatched records before calculating conversion. Distinguish unmeasured, inaccessible, pending and observed zero. Equal totals can describe different populations; a later aggregate cannot fill an earlier cohort gap.

Report conversion using its actual eligible denominator. Keep access, use, completion and attributed outcome distinct when they are the relevant stages. A documented complete case establishes that the path occurred for that case. Population-wide claims and causal credit need their own coverage or attribution evidence.

Test the strongest rival explanation, including reporting lag or changed measurement where plausible. Name the missing join or observation that could distinguish it and what result would change the owner's claim. The owner defines the stages, accepted claim and decision rule.

## Return a proposed finding

For each affected criterion, give the previous claim, new evidence, what changed and why, proposed conclusion and what would reopen review. Distinguish material change, no material change within inspected coverage and insufficient evidence. Explain coverage that limits the finding, and preserve search methods when needed to interpret or reproduce the study; an unchanged conclusion alone calls for no new record. An incomplete route that could hide a decision-changing observation remains unresolved.

Deliver a readable brief in the subject’s requested chat or page: what was learned, why it matters to the current work, the proposed consequence, the strongest counter-case or uncertainty, and the next observation worth seeking. Keep observations, inferences and proposals distinct. Explain useful learning even when no accepted conclusion changes, and ask for a decision only when a real owner choice remains. The next research step is not an assignment to the reader.

Refine the existing owning research with useful findings and source references, following the evidence guide through affected claims and uses. When the watch calls for a dated result, save it in the subject’s configured home and connect any changed interpretation to the living account. Keep storage mechanics, hashes and diagnostic output out of the brief unless a particular fact explains a practical blocker. The owner controls the accepted version and follow-on action; a scheduled research run cannot accept its own proposal.

A failure to save or deliver a result is separate from a failure to research the question. Preserve completed findings through an authorised available route, state what was saved and what was not, and resume the failed storage or delivery step without repeating research merely to obtain a successful write. Never claim a note is saved before checking it. If the subject changed while delivery was delayed, reassess the affected claims before presenting them as current. Keep unresolved research gaps visible separately from those operational failures.

## Before handing off

- [ ] Are the accepted claim, version, sources and criteria for change identified?
- [ ] Was new evidence read for what it establishes, with origins, corrections and stage measurements distinguished?
- [ ] Are revised conclusions limited to affected claims, with inspected coverage and gaps visible?
- [ ] Does the owner retain the decision to accept the update and its follow-on actions?
