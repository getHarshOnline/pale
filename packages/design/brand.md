# Brand discovery

Brand discovery resolves what an organization is trying to mean before design, naming, campaign, or copy decisions harden around assumptions. Use it when the identity is implicit, contested, inherited, or too generic to guide trade-offs. Do not use it merely to decorate an already fixed proposition or to replace market, customer, legal, or product evidence.

## Start from the decision and sources

Begin with the decision the eventual brandbook must support, who participates and who can settle it, the existing sources, the audiences and unresolved tensions. Treat stakeholder answers as attributed accounts. Compare them with customer language, product behavior, commercial constraints and historical artifacts, which may confirm or contradict them. The project owns participant identities, transcripts, confidentiality and saved interview records; this method guides the inquiry and synthesis.

At a pause, put enough context to resume in the existing interview record or conversation: the current question, answered themes, supporting examples, contradictions, thin claims, themes where further probing adds nothing, open themes and the next question. Return it in the reply when no saved record was requested. Returning that context does not establish that a file was saved, delivered or accepted.

## Inquiry sequence

Work one question at a time so each answer can change the next probe. Start with concrete episodes: when the organization was unmistakably itself, when it disappointed itself, what customers chose it instead of, and what it refuses to become. Move from event to interpretation to consequence: what happened, why it mattered, and what decision would change if the claim were true.

Pressure-test abstractions. For a generic value, request a costly example, an observable behavior, and a counterexample. For a positioning claim, name the alternative being rejected and the audience for whom the distinction matters. Use projective questions only to unlock evidence, never as proof. Paraphrase the answer, preserve the speaker's correction, and record whether the claim is supported, disputed, or still aspirational.

Close a theme when consecutive probes add no material distinction, the remaining disagreement is explicit, and at least one concrete example anchors the conclusion. A long interview is not saturation.

## Reconciliation and synthesis

Keep individual accounts separate until their internal logic is understood. Then reconcile by locating:

- shared commitments supported by behavior;
- role-dependent differences that can coexist;
- factual conflicts requiring external evidence;
- strategic forks that require an owner decision; and
- aspirations that should remain labeled as future direction.

The candidate brandbook states the identity core, audience and promise, proof, boundaries, voice, visual implications, recognizable behaviors, anti-patterns, unresolved tensions, and evidence lineage. Every section must help a downstream creator choose between plausible alternatives. If it cannot change a decision, it is decoration and should be removed.

Stop and defer when the necessary authority is absent, confidential evidence cannot be handled safely, participants are being coerced toward consensus, claims cannot be grounded, or a product/market decision must precede identity synthesis. Re-enter with the missing owner ruling, evidence, or participant account. Keep the accepted identity and source interviews in the project that owns them; this Package supplies the reusable inquiry and synthesis method.

Finish the agreed interview scope with a candidate brandbook that distinguishes observed behaviour, agreed identity, aspirations, unanswered questions and owner choices. Preserve each stakeholder’s words and corrections separately from the synthesis.
