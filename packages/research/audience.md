# Form an audience hypothesis from discovery evidence

Identify the decision, offer, audience role, job, buying stage and channel. Separate user, budget holder and approver when their questions differ. Search wording and returned pages describe a sample of discovery behaviour. Before interpreting a gap, record the queries, date, search surface, geography, language, depth and relevant collection conditions in the analysis. Mark unavailable and unsampled groups.

For a publication, start with its purpose and the reader's inquiry. The useful outcome may be understanding an argument, finding a reference, comparing alternatives or making an enquiry. Take permitted next actions from the publication's owner. A search opportunity does not give every page a sales purpose or displace existing editorial commitments.

For each query, distinguish the question its wording suggests from what returned pages serve. Identify mixed intent and whether a destination is a guide, vendor, comparison, lead form or another type. Keep that page type separate from the buyer alternative it represents. Rankings can suggest publishers and vocabulary; they cannot establish search volume, ranking difficulty, purchase demand, switching or absence of competition. Seek the smallest additional observation needed for a stronger claim, such as measured volume, buyer evidence, adoption or a credible substitution record. Preserve differences in wording, geography and buyer role.

When a practitioner supplies keyword criteria, apply their volume, difficulty, intent and exclusion rules to the relevant data. Keep the provider, metric definition, market and observation period with the selection. A provider's estimated topic demand, a site's observed query impressions and a sampled chatbot answer measure different things.

> [!WARNING]
>
> **Check the demand calculation.** Overlapping keyword or topic estimates do not add up to unique demand, and dividing a monthly estimate by days does not establish measured daily demand. Missing observations remain unknown.

Thresholds narrow candidates; they do not establish reader fit, a ranking or a likely citation. If criteria have not been supplied, label the proposed selection as a hypothesis. Explain consequential exclusions and uncertainties in the requested analysis, without inventing figures to complete it.

Choose the organising question by the task the page should help its reader complete. Group wording variants when they express the same need. Supporting questions supply the explanation, conditions, evidence or next step needed to answer that main question; a distinct task may need its own destination. The most frequent phrase need not be the organising question. Check the proposed grouping against the sampled results and audience evidence, including contrary examples. A cluster is a research hypothesis about related needs, not automatically a publication category, collection or ordered journey.

Hand off the audience and organising question, sample coverage, plausible gaps, supporting and opposing evidence, and a proposed message or page. Explain which questions belong within it and which deserve a linked destination. A brief clarification can be a paragraph; a comparison may need a table; a substantial supporting explanation may need a section. Use an FAQ for genuine recurring questions that fit the page. Neither every query nor every wording variant needs a heading. State the proof and practical next step its destination needs. One page can serve different questions when each role finds its evidence and its purpose or offer fits. Propose separate pages when audience, task, offer or proof differs enough to justify them. Editorial priority, readiness to publish, measured performance and permission to use client evidence stay with their owners. Refresh dated samples before relying on them for a new decision.

## Before handing off

- [ ] Are the audience, intended outcome, sampled discovery conditions and any publication purpose explicit?
- [ ] Does the organising question explain the grouping, with observed evidence, estimates and hypotheses distinguished?
- [ ] Does the proposed message or page name its supporting proof, missing observations and the decisions still with its owner?
