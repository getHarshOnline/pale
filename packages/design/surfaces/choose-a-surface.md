# Give surfaces a material and depth relationship

## Give material a job and construct its relationships

Choose a surface by what it supports: sustained reading, a contained image, working controls, a floating tool or a staged scene. Repeating paper grain, glass, metal and glowing accents on every page is not a material system. Equally, a surface need not demonstrate every possible material. Reuse the established roles where they serve the task and preserve deliberate exceptions.

For a coherent environmental model, relate highlights, cast shadows and edges to its actual light and geometry. A raised plane can use a tight contact edge, softer ambient cast and tonal separation; the useful combination depends on the surface and mode. An unexplained colored halo does not automatically establish depth. Inspect the rendered geometry: a rectangular shadow around an irregular shape can reveal the wrong construction. Choose the shape-aware or box-based mechanism and verify its clipping, layering and cost.

A credible luminous mark differs from a flat accent by its local luminance, falloff and relationship to its surrounding bed. When that effect is intended, judge its core and spread before relying on hue. Keep bloom from washing out adjacent text or expanding emphasis across the entire page. A photographic highlight, decorative motif and live-state emitter retain their distinct roles; there is no compulsory glow recipe or one-light limit for every scene.

Treat the supported light and dark presentations deliberately. Tone, edge and shadow contribute differently on different grounds. Compare the actual raised surfaces and their hierarchy rather than applying a palette inversion and assuming depth survived. Preserve the project’s identity and the meaning of its states in both modes. A different key or material response can be appropriate; the project owns whether its illumination direction must remain fixed.

Shared material roles and recurring construction belong in the system. An accepted correction at that level should reach actual consumers rather than becoming a new local gradient or shadow on each page. Authored scene lighting, photography and asset appearance remain with their source and production owners; do not relight them merely to match a page's surrounding frame.

## Build the surface for the job it has

A matte reading ground supports sustained information. An optical plate can hold an image or a concentrated event. An instrument plane can carry an actionable tool. A small emitter can report actual state. A page can rely almost entirely on one of these. Check whether the reader can distinguish the jobs from the placement, material and available action.

Layer density, lift and sheen only when they help an optical surface respond to its bed. Keep a translucent layer over a known, readable ground. Where two floating layers meet, use an opaque treatment or replace the lower layer. Give shadows a direction, contact and ambient spread consistent with the key. A colored ring with no offset is a halo, which cannot carry the same depth. If refraction belongs to a lens or glass form, confine noticeable distortion to the edges and check text on the worst composite. Give reduced transparency and unsupported filters an opaque treatment with the same controls and labels.

Judge hierarchy once with the material effect removed. The label, grouping and action should still be understandable; restore the material when it adds the intended character or depth. Treat ink on a reading surface as pigment unless the brief deliberately calls for luminous type. A glow around every word changes the surface into emitted light and can erase the difference between reading, emphasis and actual state.

## Worked example

Paper carries running text. A raised control uses a close contact edge and a softer cast in the key direction. A shaped marker needs a shadow that follows its silhouette, including at clipped edges. Turn off the halo in a capture to see whether the edge and tone already explain depth. In dark, raise the upper surface enough to separate it and judge the depth on the actual ground rather than inverting the colors.
