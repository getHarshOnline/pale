# Compare independent views of a consequential choice

Use a council when a consequential choice could change after hearing people with different responsibilities, access to evidence or understanding of the consequences. Routine, deterministic, inexpensive or already-authorised decisions can follow the direct path through their responsible owner. Similar opinions add little when they repeat the same reasoning. Here, council names a method for group decisions; it has no dependency on the Counsel product.

## Frame the choice and hear independent positions

Set the question, accountable owner, options, constraints, available evidence, decision criteria, deadline and reasons to reopen the question. Choose the fewest perspectives needed to expose the real tension. Each earns its place through what it uniquely protects or observes.

Each participant first forms an independent position: assumptions, evidence, strongest argument, material downside, accepted trade-off, uncertainty and what would invalidate the recommendation. Compare those positions against the same criteria. Look for whether disagreement comes from different facts, definitions, risk tolerances, time horizons or values. Voting, averaged confidence, eloquence and agreement alone cannot settle those differences.

## Distinguish a blocking objection from advice

A blocking objection identifies a permission, safety, evidence or irreversible-consequence condition that must be settled before proceeding. Separate a trade-off the accountable owner may accept from a prerequisite only another responsible party or a system check can satisfy. In the decision record, name who can settle each blocker and what evidence would do so. Keep the decision pending while that evidence is missing.

An advisory objection preserves a plausible alternative and the circumstances that would make it preferable. It carries no veto. Keep both kinds of dissent visible instead of blending them into a consensus statement.

## Return the recommendation to one decision-maker

One accountable owner makes the decision. Return the choice being considered, the perspectives, their independent positions, comparison of evidence, recommendation, reasons, accepted costs, blocking and advisory dissent, uncertainty and reasons to reconsider. Record the owner's actual choice separately when made.

Stop deliberating when the decision-maker is unclear, evidence cannot support a choice, perspectives repeat one another, or the responsible specialist or system owner must decide. State which of those applies. If one person or model considered every perspective, say that it was a single review exploring several views. Describe a meeting, an accepted decision or an action as having happened only when it did. The recommendation remains separate from the owner’s decision and permission to act.

## Before handing off

- [ ] Is the choice clear, with its decision-maker named?
- [ ] Does the record keep the strongest opposing arguments, assumptions and objections that block proceeding?
- [ ] Can the reader find the recommendation, dissent and reasons to revisit it, separately from the owner’s actual decision?
