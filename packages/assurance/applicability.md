# Research which rules apply to a proposed act

Use this method to establish whether and how a law, policy, regulation, contract or professional rule applies to a particular decision or proposed act. Start with that question rather than a general summary of the subject. Return what the evidence supports, which conclusions depend on unresolved facts and where the Package's role ends.

## Begin with the act and its coordinates

State the decision before collecting sources. Identify:

- the proposed act, decision or consequence;
- every affected person, entity, relationship, resource, instrument or transaction;
- the jurisdiction asserted by the caller and plausible competing jurisdictions;
- the event or transaction date;
- the assessment, reporting, filing or operating period if it differs from that date;
- the requested consequence and how the answer would be used;
- references to known private facts, without unnecessarily copying private content; and
- facts that are missing, disputed, stale or held by another authority.

These details define the research scope. A changed act changes the applicability question. Do not silently reuse a source collection assembled for a different act.

## Resolve a predicate map

Identify the conditions that must hold for a rule to apply. For each material condition, state whether evidence supports it, refutes it or leaves it unresolved, and cite that evidence. A source label alone cannot establish applicability.

### Authority and jurisdiction

Identify who created the rule, the territory and subject matter governed, and the facts that could bring the act within its reach. Language, currency, website domain or one participant's location alone does not establish jurisdiction. Keep overlapping, delegated and competing authorities as separate possibilities until the relevant connections are established.

### Capacity and role

Establish the capacity in which each person or entity acts. The same subject may have different duties as owner, employer, intermediary, issuer, resident, registered person, fiduciary, controller, agent, counterparty or signatory. Verify a role supplied by the caller rather than treating it as settled.

### Event classification

Describe the event's evidenced characteristics before assigning a category under a law or policy. Keep that classification separate from the commercial label in a system or contract. Preserve alternative classifications when a missing term, election, relationship or fact could change the result.

### Connecting and modifying predicates

Test the relevant conditions concerning territory, residency, registration, instrument, activity, relationship and purpose. Then check exceptions, exclusions, elections, safe harbours, special treatments and anti-avoidance or overriding provisions separately. Do not assume a favourable exception applies or apply a general rule while a plausible specific rule remains unresolved.

### Time

Establish commencement, effective-from and effective-to dates, amendments, transition rules, grandfathering, replacement of earlier rules and differences between event and decision periods. “Current” or “latest” does not identify the text governing a historical event or an evaluation already in progress.

### External dependencies

Identify conditions that depend on a contract, private record, election, registration, professional determination, court or tribunal outcome, signature or another authority's decision. Research may identify these dependencies; it cannot invent a missing fact or exercise someone else's authority.

## Select sources by the claim they must prove

Choose sources for the claim each must establish. Prestige alone is not a universal ranking:

- enacted or otherwise authoritative text establishes the rule attributed to it;
- rules and subordinate legislation may supply operative detail within delegated authority;
- Gazette notifications, commencement instruments and amendments establish changes and their timing;
- regulator directions, circulars, master circulars, orders and formal guidance may govern, clarify, administer or show the regulator's position within their stated scope;
- court or tribunal decisions may control or materially qualify an interpretation where their authority, jurisdiction and status apply;
- official forms, schemas, filing instructions and observed portal behaviour may establish how administration works, but do not by themselves change the governing rule;
- professional interpretation may explain a contested reading, but remains interpretation unless the relevant system explicitly gives it another status; and
- secondary material may orient research and reveal terminology, but cannot silently replace the source required for a claim.

Treat search results, snippets, summaries, remembered values, inherited plan literals and unofficial compilations as leads. They are not authoritative evaluation inputs. Use an approved research capability for current retrieval, retain the exact reference or captured bytes, and identify retrieval time and source owner.

## Reconstruct a coherent source chain

For each material claim, follow the base authority through amendments, commencement or effective provisions, subordinate instruments, clarifications, corrigenda and known decisions that change its reading. Identify what was replaced, what survived and what applied during the target period. Preserve source identity, retrieved bytes or a reproducible reference, retrieval date and access limitations.

Keep this chain visible rather than reducing it to one quotation. A provision may exist without having commenced. A published amendment may not govern the target period. Guidance may describe administration without settling the legal question. Show conflicts and the relationship between authorities instead of choosing the convenient text.

## Keep the reasoning layers separate

Distinguish these claims:

1. Source existence: the source and its contents can be identified.
2. Applicability: established conditions connect it to the proposed act and period.
3. Interpretation: reasoning explains what the applicable material means.
4. Calculation: an accepted implementation combines exact governing rules, facts and numerical conventions.
5. Professional approval: an appropriately qualified or authorised person accepts the required judgment.
6. Effect: a filing, payment, disclosure, allocation, trade, instruction, publication or other change is authorised and actually performed.

Evidence for one does not establish the next. A supported interpretation is not a calculation. An approved calculation is not a payment instruction. An interface response does not establish that an external obligation was discharged.

## Produce an applicability dossier

Return the findings in the existing research result or review artifact. This account supports review; it does not define an engine schema or require a separate administrative file. Include:

- the decision and proposed act;
- affected subjects and the details that determine applicability;
- each claim or question;
- sources grouped by the role they serve;
- established facts and their owners;
- interpretations and the reasoning connecting them to evidence;
- counterevidence, alternative readings and unresolved conflicts;
- dependencies on private facts;
- the conclusion about the effective period;
- uncertainty and missing evidence;
- evidence that could support a proposed set of rules next; and
- actions that remain prohibited.

State the result for each claim. Avoid one “applicable” verdict when some conditions are established and others remain unresolved.

## Abstract worked example

A cooperative considers changing how it records a member transaction begun in Period A and settling in Period B. A newly published instrument changes the treatment of that transaction class, but commencement and transition provisions refer to different dates.

The researcher identifies the recording decision, the cooperative's asserted role, transaction characteristics, both periods and desired reporting consequence. The base authority establishes the general category; the amendment changes its wording; a commencement instrument supplies one relevant date. A transition provision may preserve earlier treatment for events meeting an additional condition. The member agreement is needed to establish when the relevant right arose.

Without that agreement, the research can establish the source chain and two conditional outcomes, but cannot select one. It identifies the missing private fact and an assertion that could be proposed once the fact is established. Calculation, reporting and filing remain prohibited.

## Stop conditions

Stop with an unresolved result, or a result limited to the supported scope, when:

- jurisdiction or target period cannot be established;
- required private facts are missing, conflicting, stale or unauthorised;
- the source chain is incomplete or cannot be reproduced;
- a material amendment, transition or authority conflict cannot be reconciled;
- a professional, signatory, regulator, court, tribunal or other owner must determine the issue; or
- the request would require attestation, calculation, advice, filing, payment, disclosure, allocation, trading, publication or another action.

Stopping is a valid result. Well-supported research does not authorise the next action.
