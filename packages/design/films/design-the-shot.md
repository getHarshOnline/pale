# Stage the action and protect the reading plane

## Stage the action and the read together

Plan the focal subject or relationship, meaningful change, framing, reading plane and endpoints. A wide view can establish geography, a detail can expose the consequential response, and a return can show its context. Use camera movement when it changes the audience's understanding or serves the selected experience. A fixed camera can contain complex choreography; moving around an unchanged slide can still communicate little.

Compose coordinated parts beneath a clear attention hierarchy. A carrier, its contents, the environment and type can change together without competing. Separate property ownership where camera, object, mask and protected type need independent control. Author the launch, travel, arrival, subordinate response and readable hold. Distance, apparent weight, framing and sound inform timing; one easing or fixed stagger need not serve every property. Repetition can establish rhythm or comparison. Novelty quotas and a rule against repeating a device both evade the actual judgment.

Choose a transition for its editorial relation. An action match continues an action; a graphic match carries visual correspondence; a viewpoint change reveals another relation; a dissolve can signal elapsed time; a direct cut or deliberate contrast can be the right choice. Do not make an object perform every cut merely to supply physical continuity. Inspect eye trace, screen direction, recognizable subject and the first readable incoming state. Matched endpoints cannot establish a good intervening transformation.

In a product demonstration, show the starting state, meaningful action and visible consequence with time to register. Preserve operative labels and evidence. Avoid an editorial gesture implying a transfer or causal operation that separate captures never established. Compress a wait visibly when needed; editorial motion is not measured application progress. A label naming a feature cannot replace showing its distinguishing response.

For a comprehension problem, use an animatic or connected study at the intended scale and pace before expensive production. Where useful, ask a reader to retell the action and consequence without the author's explanation. Cover explanatory copy while preserving functional text that is itself the subject; then compare the complete version. Distinguish what they observed, inferred or recovered only from a label. This is a targeted diagnostic, not a mandatory ceremony for every edit. A single clear shot does not prove a whole sequence requirement. Give the opening, central change, adjacent holds and payoff their own scrutiny in connected playback.

Choose among the constructions in [Choose a construction for the visible change](choose-a-mechanism.md), then record the chosen treatment and its simpler alternative in [Plan a shot from both ends](frame-a-shot.md).

## Worked example

A sequence shows one component entering a container. Establish the two shapes, show the moving component passing behind the edge, then hold on the result long enough to read. Keep the caption in a stable plane. Watch the connected cut and ask a reader what changed. If they infer a transfer the recording never showed, change the edit or wording.
