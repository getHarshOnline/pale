# Research a market for a decision

Define the market around the decision it must support. Use the research guide to judge claims, source independence, contradictions and confidence; this guide supplies the market model. Retrieve current public evidence through a permitted tool. If access is missing, keep the gap visible rather than inventing a market size.

## Define what is being counted

Name the decision owner, choice, available time and threshold that would change the choice. Define the product, service, job or outcome exchanged; counting unit such as people, accounts, sites, transactions, units, capacity or revenue; geography and period; buyer, user, payer and channel; eligibility and exclusions; and alternatives, including doing nothing, internal provision and less obvious substitutes.

Every estimate must answer to those definitions. Global revenue, local customer counts and a vendor's pipeline are not comparable just because they share a category name.

## Examine demand and supply

For demand, ask who experiences the need, how often and urgently, with what budget, decision power and switching behaviour. Distinguish expressed interest, purchase, adoption, retention and repeat use. For supply, ask who can serve the need, at what capacity, through which channels, with what economics, constraints and response time. Distribution, regulation, implementation capacity or supply may limit reachable demand.

Create a segment when it changes attractiveness, route to market, product requirements, price, risk or confidence. A descriptive grouping that changes no action adds no decision value.

## Reconcile estimates

When sizing matters, use at least two different lines of reasoning. A top-down estimate narrows a defensible total population through explicit eligibility, adoption and value filters. A bottom-up estimate starts from observable units, achievable acquisition or capacity, use frequency and value per unit.

In the analysis, show each equation, unit, source and assumption. The eligible universe, serviceable market and obtainable near-term result answer different questions. Explain differences between estimates. If they cannot be reconciled to the same unit and period, keep them separate and state which decision each supports.

Use ranges for uncertain inputs. Find which assumptions change the conclusion and the value at which the preferred choice switches. Focus further research on those variables.

## Challenge the explanation

Distinguish current signals from lasting conditions. A recent spike may fade; a short series may hide a durable constraint. Test category mistakes, channel displacement, buyer-payer mismatch, hidden substitutes, supply bottlenecks and changed measurement. Seek observations that could disprove the opportunity.

State comparability limits: definitions, currencies, nominal versus real values, firms versus establishments, overlapping segments, survey populations and time windows can make precise-looking figures impossible to add.

Stop when the decision holds across plausible values, a specific unknown has been handed off for measurement or further precision cannot change the choice.

## Turn an upstream finding into a buyer question

Carry the source claim, status, date, evidence limits and permitted use into the research handoff. Name the receiving buyer or segment, workflow and commercial decision. Frame the implication as a testable question. A mechanism observed elsewhere supports this case only as far as its applicability is established.

Trace the proposed connection from the finding to a buyer-controlled action or economic event. Identify where new evidence from this workflow is needed. For the same buyer, period and decision, test the strongest rival explanation and a competent current alternative. For example, an apparent integration gap might instead come from contract terms or buyer approval. If both explanations fit, name the smallest observation that could distinguish them.

Distinguish a research question, a prospect supported by evidence, a paid commitment and an accepted outcome. Check what a payment actually bought before attributing it to another offer. Research alone cannot approve a prospect, change an offer, permit outreach or authorise public proof. Give proposed changes to the responsible owner with sources and remaining conditions. Keep any later decision, its scope and evidence with that owner, alongside the source's unchanged status.

## Test the workflow gap and paid next step

For an account or segment, identify the recurring workflow, owner of the economic outcome, budget holder, trigger and acceptance event that changes that outcome. Fix population, period and measurement unit. Where applicable, distinguish recorded completion, submitted evidence, accepted performance, billability, invoicing and settlement. Join the owning systems' records to measure loss, delay or cost between stages. Separate unmatched, late and unknown records from actual losses.

Compare a competently configured incumbent with the proposal using the same workflow, access, outcomes and support scope. Test whether configuration, integration, an operating change or doing nothing would suffice. A vendor feature is a capability claim; an account report supports only its observed or reported scope. Before attributing a gap to integration, inspect the account's configuration and control for contract terms, volume, approval rules, staffing and data quality. Identify who can change the controlling step.

Keep problem interviews, an accountable buyer's budget and timing, paid diagnostic commitment, delivered work, accepted outcome and voluntary repeat use distinct in the analysis. Use a limited diagnostic to choose between alternatives, with evidence that would stop it. Attribute payment and benefit to the offer purchased. A paid service can establish demand for that service while standalone demand for a component remains untested. Compare access, liability, delivery and support effort with available capacity before advancing the case.

Return the strongest alternative explanation, smallest distinguishing observation, proposed next check and authorised owner. Account approval, outreach, pricing, implementation and public proof remain separate decisions. Before wider rollout, seek independent evidence that the result transfers and revisit the economics.

## Worked example

An owner is considering discovery for scheduling software sold to regional repair firms over the next year. Operations managers buy, dispatchers use, the firm pays, and spreadsheets and existing enterprise software are substitutes.

A top-down estimate starts with an official firm count, filters geography, employee band and observable workflow fit, then applies an explicitly hypothetical annual value. A bottom-up estimate uses reachable channels, sales capacity, a conversion range, implementation capacity and the same annual value. A large eligible market with few obtainable customers points to distribution or onboarding capacity as the constraint on the one-year decision.

Test whether a small pilot can acquire and implement enough representative firms under a pessimistic conversion assumption. If so, fund discovery with a stopping condition; otherwise reject or redesign the route to market. Any figures supplied later remain dated evidence. The reusable method is defining and reconciling the estimates.

## Return the market assessment

In the analysis, return the market definition, equations and ranges, sources and assumptions, conflicting evidence, sensitivity and switching conditions, segments that change the action, and a scoped recommendation. Identify dated facts needing refresh. Stop when the choice holds across plausible values or a critical unknown has been isolated for measurement.

Customer contact, commissioned research, data purchases, publication and changes to a commercial plan each need the appropriate tool and approval beyond this analysis.

## Before handing off

- [ ] Are the decision, alternatives, counting unit and period clear?
- [ ] Are observed facts, assumptions, estimates and interpretation distinct and traceable?
- [ ] Does the result show the rival explanations, switching conditions and evidence needed for the next decision?
