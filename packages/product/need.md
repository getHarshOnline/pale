# Find the need behind a product request

Decide whether a proposed change addresses a sufficiently understood need before treating its suggested solution as the thing to build.

## Understand the problem and the alternatives

Find who experiences the problem, in what situation, what they are trying to do, how they cope today and what shows its frequency or consequence. Distinguish a reported preference from a difficulty you observed, one customer's constraint from a recurring product need, and a requested solution from the product owner's decision.

Compare other explanations for the observations, then compare the proposal with simpler changes, changes to how the work is done, existing product abilities and doing nothing. Describe the expected user or business outcome in observable terms and what would show the premise is wrong. Include costs the proposal shifts onto other users, operators or the product's future consistency.

## Return the next decision

In your assessment, recommend proceeding, investigating, reframing or declining. Include the supported problem statement, affected people or systems, alternatives considered, evidence about the outcome and unresolved assumptions. Finish when the next decision can be made from the need and its evidence. Roadmap priority, budget and implementation design remain decisions for their owners.

## Worked example

Users ask for a new status dashboard. Observe how often they leave their current task to find out whether a request finished and what they do when the status is unclear. Compare a status link in the existing view with a new dashboard. Decide what to pursue based on whether people can find the result and resume their task.

## Before handing off

- [ ] Does the assessment identify the person, task, observed problem and current workaround?
- [ ] Can the reader distinguish observed behaviour from requested solutions and proposed value?
- [ ] Have other plausible explanations and alternatives been compared with the evidence?
- [ ] Is there a small question or experiment that could change the recommendation, with the next decision left to its owner?
