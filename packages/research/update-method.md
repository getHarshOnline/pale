# Compare new evidence with an accepted baseline

## Establish the accepted claim

Identify the claim and baseline version, owner, previous evidence, conditions that make a change material and possible decisions. Distinguish the accepted position, proposed interpretations and earlier observations. Before screening new material, state what would strengthen, narrow, contradict or leave the claim intact. If the baseline cannot be identified, preserve the new evidence and hold the update.

## Compare source changes and applicability

Distinguish an independent origin, a revision or correction to the same origin, and a new location or commentary. Identify each revision and compare the actual passage, population, units, time and method. A correction can invalidate a claim without adding an independent source. In the review, identify claims that relied on superseded evidence and the correction's scope.

Compare direct follow-up with the strongest available contradictory or null finding. Examine neglected populations, alternative explanations or a plausible reverse causal direction when they could change the decision. Use another domain's work when the mapping and a test that could disprove it are explicit. Novelty, recency, citation counts and resemblance alone cannot change an accepted claim. Identify gaps and inaccessible counterevidence.

## Connect evidence across the claimed stages

Trace observed input to the decision-relevant outcome. For each transition, define what counts as reaching the next stage and identify entity, event, observation window, unit and denominator. A stage label establishes the wording used. Check its definition and underlying records before treating a new label on the same number as movement through the process.

Join successive stages with stable identifiers or independently checked linkage. Align eligibility, time windows, weights and measurement unit. Account for one-to-many events, repeated observations, expiry and unmatched records before calculating conversion. Distinguish unmeasured, inaccessible, pending and observed zero. Equal totals can describe different populations; a later aggregate cannot fill an earlier cohort gap.

Report conversion using its actual eligible denominator. Keep access, use, completion and attributed outcome distinct when they are the relevant stages. A documented complete case establishes that the path occurred for that case. Population-wide claims and causal credit need their own coverage or attribution evidence.

Test the strongest rival explanation, including reporting lag or changed measurement where plausible. Name the missing join or observation that could distinguish it and what result would change the owner's claim. The owner defines the stages, accepted claim and decision rule.

## Return a proposed finding

For each affected criterion, give the previous claim, new evidence, what changed and why, proposed conclusion and what would reopen review. Distinguish material change, no material change within inspected coverage and insufficient evidence. Preserve the search coverage even when the conclusion stands. An incomplete route that could hide a decision-changing observation remains unresolved.

Deliver the assessment for the owner to accept or reject. That owner controls the accepted version, history, records and follow-on actions. Scheduling collection or updating an accepted store are separate tasks.
