# Find the evidence a question still needs

> This retained predecessor is superseded by [Find the evidence a question still needs](sources.md). Use that method for current work; the per-query diary below is no longer required.

Search in successive rounds when the necessary material cannot be known from the initial context. Retrieval finds possible evidence; the research guide judges what it supports.

Start with the claim, the evidence needed to decide it and the smallest authoritative source likely to answer it. In the existing research record, keep each query's unresolved question, search location, terms or identifiers, time range and reason it could change the answer. After each round, distinguish findings, contradictions, absence, inaccessible material and stale results.

Let the remaining gap determine the next query. Change source type, definition, period or method deliberately. Similar queries over one source history add no independence. Summaries and indexes help navigation; inspect the original source before relying on a consequential claim.

In the research handoff, give the search path, coverage, failed routes, access limits, gaps and reason to stop. Finish when the decision has enough support, a named external observation is needed or further retrieval cannot change the result.

If a route is unavailable, retain the query or source needed, claims left open and any supplied snapshot supporting a narrower dated finding. Search repositories or local indexes within the permitted scope. Use a current provider only when connected and appropriate. Keep private names, excerpts, secrets and unpublished assumptions out of external queries and connectors.

## Follow an index to the saved source

Read the identified item before citing it. In the research record, identify the original source and revision when known, observation date, saved item's identity and location, and whether it contains original bytes, an extract or an analyst's summary. An extract's digest identifies the extract; mark claims that need the original.

When a location moves, verify the saved identity before updating the link. When content changes, identify a separate revision and compare the relevant evidence. Preserve dated observations and connect corrections to affected claims. Name an unresolved saved item as missing. An authorised current lookup can add a new observation, but cannot reconstruct the earlier capture.
