# Build a board that leads to decisions

Gather existing references before generating examples when the question is open. Look across disciplines for materials and light, and inspect actual products or sequences for behavior. A board frames what needs deciding, aligns the people making it, holds useful tensions, turns observed work into usable qualities and gives the next stage a direction. Starting from one generated image can fix later choices on its style.

Choose a central image that carries the intended character. Give the other images a job beside it. Ten to fifteen images and two to five mood words can be a starting size for exploration. A reconstruction can use one actual source and more detail. Keep the references in the project source library. For each, record its origin, role, quality, placement, and permission to use or upload it. Check the hosted tool's rules before sending a private image. A provider's board code is a derivative. Keep the readable board and references under the project's control.

When the direction is open, compare two or three coherent boards on named differences such as material, illumination or human presence. Write the desired quality and the tempting wrong reading in concrete terms. Three to six pairs may help a broad direction. After agreement, a facet board for type, light or people develops the shared choice. An approved reference, reconstruction or reusable source goes directly to the work it supports. It need not go back through open exploration.

Use the agreed board to make cheap tests of the chosen territory if needed. Give a generated set repeatable decisions for medium, light, palette, material, lens and recurring subjects. For a film, carry the applicable direction and the original references into the shot decisions. Compare the resulting work with the reference's role. Inspiration should show kinship without copying its source.

## Worked example

| Reference | Role and placement | Quality to carry | Permission |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Project's own close-up of a worn instrument | Center | Fine abrasion catches a raking key while engraved values stay clear | Project can reuse |
| Paper atlas opened to a sparse chart | Range | One dominant measure, thin annotation | Inspect locally before any upload |
| Existing project demo | Implementation source for button feedback | A change of light marks real state without displacing the label | Reuse from the maintained source |

Try weathered metal with stable lettering against smooth enamel with the same hierarchy. Judge which helps the subject read. Record the chosen material and key in the design brief rather than asking the generator to imitate the whole board.
