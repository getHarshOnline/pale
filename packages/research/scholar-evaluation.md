# Evaluate a scholarly work

Review the argument from question through evidence to conclusion. Identify the artifact version, corrections or retractions, claimed contribution, study context, availability of data and materials, and decision the review informs.

In the review, map primary and secondary claims to their evidence and methodological assumptions, showing where interpretation exceeds observation. Check whether the design can identify the claimed relationship; whether sampling, measurement, controls, timing and comparison groups fit; and whether analysis handles missing data, multiple comparisons, uncertainty and plausible rival explanations. Conclusions should preserve those limits.

Inspect what citations support, their independence and whether they are represented fairly. Assess reproducibility from available protocol, data, code, parameters and environment. Distinguish unavailable materials from demonstrated failure to reproduce.

Seek robustness evidence suited to the method: alternative specifications, sensitivity, negative controls, replication, held-out evaluation or triangulation. Use techniques that answer the actual concern. Identify conventional work, new contribution and whether the contribution survives a narrower defensible claim.

Return the claim map, design assessment, evidence strengths and gaps, citation findings, reproducibility, robustness, limitations, contribution judgment and concrete revisions or follow-up evidence. For each material claim, choose supported, supported with limits, unresolved or unsupported. Keep those judgments separate rather than averaging them into a prestige score.

## Before handing off

- [ ] Do methods, results and sources support the claims?
- [ ] Is it clear what can be reproduced and where the claim stops?
- [ ] Does the feedback identify gaps that change interpretation?
