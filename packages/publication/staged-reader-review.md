# Check what the reader can infer at each stage

Identify the candidate version, intended order, audience knowledge and what should be clear or remain open at each checkpoint. Read in that order without later answers or the creator's explanation. In the review, record what the reader can point to, what they infer, where they get lost and what is revealed too early. For reading order, page turns or navigation, use the intended format. A list of beats checks the plan; the actual sequence shows what reaches the reader.

When a claim depends on a cue, make a separate variant withholding only that cue. Keep other context and prompts comparable, then compare with the complete version. State whether the cue was necessary for comprehension, confirmed an already supported inference or came with other changes. Distinguish changes in sequence, language, presentation and evaluation criteria. A first-read claim needs someone who has not seen the answer.

Tie findings to evidence in the candidate. Distinguish deliberate ambiguity, unintended spoilers and missing context. This review answers the stated reader question; accessibility, complete presentation and medium-specific delivery need their own checks. The owner determines story, required clues, thresholds and permitted ambiguity.

## Before handing off

- [ ] Did the reader receive only what was available at that stage?
- [ ] Were inferences captured before later answers were shown?
- [ ] Did the intended clue or reveal work?
