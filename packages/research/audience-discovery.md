# Form an audience hypothesis from discovery evidence

Identify the decision, offer, audience role, job, buying stage and channel. Separate user, budget holder and approver when their questions differ. Search wording and returned pages describe a sample of discovery behaviour. Before interpreting a gap, record the queries, date, search surface, geography, language, depth and relevant collection conditions in the analysis. Mark unavailable and unsampled groups.

For each query, distinguish the question its wording suggests from what returned pages serve. Identify mixed intent and whether a destination is a guide, vendor, comparison, lead form or another type. Keep that page type separate from the buyer alternative it represents. Rankings can suggest publishers and vocabulary; they cannot establish search volume, ranking difficulty, purchase demand, switching or absence of competition. Seek the smallest additional observation needed for a stronger claim, such as measured volume, buyer evidence, adoption or a credible substitution record. Preserve differences in wording, geography and buyer role.

Hand off the audience and question, sample coverage, plausible gaps, supporting and opposing evidence, and a proposed message or page. State the proof and practical next step its destination needs. One page can serve different questions when each role finds its evidence and the same offer fits. Propose separate pages when audience, offer or proof differs enough to justify them. Editorial priority, readiness to publish, measured performance and permission to use client evidence stay with their owners. Refresh dated samples before relying on them for a new decision.
